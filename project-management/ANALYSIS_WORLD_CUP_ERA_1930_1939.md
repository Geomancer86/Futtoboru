## 🏛️ Executive Summary

The 1930s is arguably the most important decade for international football. It saw the birth of the **FIFA World Cup**, the rise of the first true tactical "School" (the Austrian **Wunderteam**), and the technological leap from radio to **Television (1937)**. The decade ends with the economic scars of the Great Depression and the global suspension of football in 1939 due to WWII.

## 2. TACTICAL EVOLUTION: METODO VS. WM

Managers in this decade must choose between two dominant philosophies:

*   **The Metodo (2-3-2-3):** Vittorio Pozzo's system. Focuses on defensive solidity and quick counters. Used by the 2x World Champion Italy.
*   **The WM (3-2-2-3):** Herbert Chapman's refined system. Now becoming the global standard for English-influenced teams.
*   **The "Danubian School":** Short passing and high technicality (Austria, Hungary).

## 3. MEDIA & COMMUNICATION (1930-1939)

### 3.1 The Television Revolution (1937)
*   **The Milestone:** Sep 16, 1937 (Arsenal vs Arsenal Reserves).
*   **Gameplay Impact:** 
    *   **"Televised Match" Bonus:** Home teams start receiving small "TV Appearance" fees (early commercialization).
    *   **Inbox News:** High-quality photography and "Tele-Reports" replace simple telegrams.

### 3.2 Press & Photography
*   **Action Photography:** Match reports now feature "staged" or action photos, making the inbox feel more like a modern newspaper.
*   **International Correspondence:** Papers like *L'Équipe* (growing from *L'Auto*) send reporters globally for the World Cups. Information lag for World Cup scores is now **zero** thanks to improved cable/radio.

## 4. THE GREAT DEPRESSION & ECONOMICS

*   **Financial Impact:** (1930-1935)
    *   **Gate Receipts:** Drop by 20-30% in industrial cities.
    *   **Salaries:** Implementation of more rigid "Maximum Wage" rules to prevent club bankruptcy.
    *   **Transfers:** The "Big Money" transfers slow down; clubs rely more on local youth development.
