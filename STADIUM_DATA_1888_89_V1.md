## Stadium Data Compilation

### 1. Preston North End - Deepdale

**Stadium Name:** Deepdale  
**Built Year:** 1875 (first football match: 1878)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 10,000  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £4,000-6,000  
**Ownership:** Owned (leased from 1875)  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,000  
**Annual Rent:** £0 (owned)  

**Historical Summary:**
Deepdale is the oldest continuously used professional football stadium in the world. Originally Deepdale Farm, the land was leased on January 21, 1875 by the town's North End sports club, initially used for cricket and rugby. The first association football match was played there on October 5, 1878. Preston North End was founded in 1880 and became a founding member of the Football League in 1888, achieving the "Double" (league and FA Cup) in the inaugural season.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepdale

### 2. Aston Villa - Wellington Road

**Stadium Name:** Wellington Road (Perry Barr)  
**Built Year:** 1876  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 26,849 (record attendance, Jan 7, 1888)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £5,000-7,000  
**Ownership:** Likely rented/leased  
**Land Value:** £2,000-3,000  
**Annual Rent:** £150-250 (estimated)  

**Historical Summary:**
Wellington Road was Aston Villa's home from 1876 to 1897. Initially lacked spectator facilities, with players changing in a nearby blacksmith's shed. Improvements included a grandstand on the eastern touchline and pavilions. Hosted record crowd of 26,849 for FA Cup match against Preston North End in January 1888. The first league match at Wellington Road was on September 15, 1888, with Villa defeating Stoke 5-1 in front of 2,000 spectators. Moved to Villa Park in 1897 due to uneven pitch and increasing crowd sizes.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Road_(Perry_Barr)

### 3. Wolverhampton Wanderers - Dudley Road (1888), Molineux (1889)

**Stadium Name (1888):** Dudley Road  
**Stadium Name (1889+):** Molineux  
**Built Year:** Dudley Road: 1881, Molineux: 1889  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 10,000 (Dudley Road), 4,000+ (Molineux initial)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £3,000-5,000 (Dudley Road), £6,000-8,000 (Molineux)  
**Ownership:** Rented (Dudley Road), Leased (Molineux from Northampton Brewery)  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,500  
**Annual Rent:** £200-300 (estimated)  

**Historical Summary:**
Wolves played at Dudley Road from 1881 to 1889. First Football League match at Dudley Road was September 8, 1888, a 1-1 draw against Aston Villa. Record attendance was 10,000 for FA Cup match against The Wednesday in March 1889. In 1889, moved to Molineux, leased from Northampton Brewery Company. Molineux became the first stadium in England built specifically for a football team. First league match at Molineux was September 7, 1889, Wolves beating Notts County 2-0 in front of 4,000 spectators.

**Wikipedia Links:** 
- Dudley Road: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Road
- Molineux: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molineux_Stadium

### 4. Blackburn Rovers - Ewood Park

**Stadium Name:** Ewood Park (Ewood Bridge)  
**Built Year:** 1882 (opened April 1882)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 10,000+  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £6,000-9,000  
**Ownership:** Rented initially, purchased freehold in 1893 for £2,500  
**Land Value:** £2,000-3,000  
**Annual Rent (1888):** £200-300 (estimated, before purchase)  

**Historical Summary:**
Ewood Park (originally Ewood Bridge) opened in April 1882 as a multi-purpose sports venue hosting football, athletics, and greyhound racing. Blackburn Rovers played several matches there in 1882 before moving in permanently in 1890. First match at Ewood Park was September 1890 against Accrington, a 0-0 draw attended by 10,000 spectators. The club purchased the freehold in 1893 for £2,500. Featured horse boxes, dressing tents, refreshment saloon, lavatories, and a grandstand with natural elevation.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewood_Park

### 5. Bolton Wanderers - Pike's Lane

**Stadium Name:** Pike's Lane  
**Built Year:** 1880  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 5,000-10,000  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £3,000-5,000  
**Ownership:** Likely rented  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,000  
**Annual Rent:** £150-250 (estimated)  

**Historical Summary:**
Pike's Lane was Bolton Wanderers' home from 1880 until 1895. Initially featured embankments on both sides, later developments included a grandstand on the northern touchline and wooden terracing behind the eastern goal. On September 8, 1888, hosted one of the inaugural Football League matches, Bolton losing 6-3 to Derby County in front of 5,000 spectators. Kenny Davenport scored the first-ever goal in Football League history just two minutes into the game. Record attendance was 20,000 for FA Cup match against Liverpool in February 1894. Often criticized for poor pitch conditions and inadequate facilities. Moved to Burnden Park in 1895.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike%27s_Lane

### 6. West Bromwich Albion - Stoney Lane

**Stadium Name:** Stoney Lane  
**Built Year:** 1885 (first match: September 5, 1885)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 2,100-20,977  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £4,000-6,000  
**Ownership:** Leased (7-year lease from 1885)  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,500  
**Annual Rent:** £28 (from Mr. H. Webb, local undertaker)  

**Historical Summary:**
Stoney Lane served as West Bromwich Albion's home from 1885 to 1900. Secured a seven-year lease for £28 per annum from local undertaker Mr. H. Webb. Pitch was returfed and ashes spread around perimeter. A wooden grandstand known as "Noah's Ark" was constructed along the southern touchline, accommodating approximately 600 spectators under cover. First league match at Stoney Lane was September 29, 1888, Albion defeating Burnley 4-3 in front of 2,100 spectators. Record league attendance was 19,700 in January 1895. Moved to The Hawthorns in 1900.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoney_Lane

### 7. Accrington - Thorneyholme Road

**Stadium Name:** Thorneyholme Road  
**Built Year:** 1877 (cricket club), 1878 (football club)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 4,000-10,000  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £2,500-4,000  
**Ownership:** Shared with cricket club (likely rented)  
**Land Value:** £1,000-1,500  
**Annual Rent:** £100-200 (estimated, shared with cricket)  

**Historical Summary:**
Thorneyholme Road was shared by Accrington Cricket Club (since 1877) and Accrington Football Club (founded 1878, known as "Owd Reds"). The football pitch was at the western end, featuring a covered stand and uncovered seating along the western touchline, plus seating behind both goals. First league match was October 6, 1888, a 4-4 draw against Wolverhampton Wanderers in front of 4,000 spectators. Record league attendance was 10,000 for local derby against Blackburn Rovers in January 1893. Accrington resigned from Football League after 1892-93 season rather than be relegated. The area that once hosted the football pitch is now occupied by tennis courts.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorneyholme_Road

### 8. Everton - Anfield

**Stadium Name:** Anfield  
**Built Year:** 1884 (Everton moved there), 1885 (Houlding purchased)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 10,000+  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £6,000 (purchase price in 1885)  
**Ownership:** Owned by John Houlding (brewer), Everton rented  
**Land Value:** £2,000-3,000  
**Annual Rent:** £100 (1888), increased to £240 (1891)  

**Historical Summary:**
Everton moved to Anfield in 1884, ground owned by John Orrell (friend of club president John Houlding). In 1885, Houlding purchased Anfield from Orrell for approximately £6,000 (£2,000 own funds, £4,000 mortgage at 3% interest). Rent increased from £100 (1888) to £240 (1891), causing tensions. Everton left Anfield in 1892 to move to Goodison Park. Houlding then founded Liverpool FC to occupy the vacant Anfield. First Liverpool match at Anfield was September 3, 1892.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield

### 9. Burnley - Turf Moor

**Stadium Name:** Turf Moor  
**Built Year:** 1883 (Burnley FC moved there)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 5,800+ (800 seated, 5,000 standing)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £3,500-5,500  
**Ownership:** Shared with cricket club (likely rented)  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,500  
**Annual Rent:** £150-250 (estimated, shared with cricket)  

**Historical Summary:**
Turf Moor has been associated with sports since at least 1843 when Burnley Cricket Club relocated there. In 1883, the cricket club invited Burnley FC to move from Calder Vale to a pitch adjacent to the cricket field. First match at Turf Moor was February 17, 1883 (lost 6-3 to Rawtenstall). In 1885, constructed an 800-seat wooden grandstand along the south side and added uncovered standing for 5,000 at each end. In 1886, became first football ground visited by a member of the Royal Family (Prince Albert Victor). First league match was October 6, 1888, Burnley defeating Bolton Wanderers 4-1. Fred Poland scored the first league goal at Turf Moor just five minutes into the game.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turf_Moor

### 10. Derby County - County Cricket Ground (1888), Baseball Ground (1895)

**Stadium Name (1888):** County Cricket Ground  
**Stadium Name (1895+):** Baseball Ground  
**Built Year:** County Ground: pre-1884, Baseball Ground: 1890  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 5,000-8,000 (estimated)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £3,000-5,000 (County Ground)  
**Ownership:** Rented (County Ground), Leased (Baseball Ground)  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,500  
**Annual Rent:** £150-250 (estimated)  

**Historical Summary:**
Derby County, established in 1884, initially played at the County Cricket Ground. Due to fixture clashes with cricket, they occasionally used Ley's Baseball Ground (established 1890 by Sir Francis Ley) for home games. Made Baseball Ground their permanent home in 1895, remaining there for 102 years. The Baseball Ground was renowned for its unique atmosphere, especially during night matches when nearby foundry operations added to the ambiance. However, the pitch was notorious for poor condition due to drainage issues (located below street level).

**Wikipedia Links:**
- Baseball Ground: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Ground_(Derby)
- Derby County: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_County_F.C.

### 11. Notts County - Trent Bridge

**Stadium Name:** Trent Bridge  
**Built Year:** 1883 (Notts County began playing there)  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 8,000-12,000 (estimated)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £4,000-6,000 (shared with cricket)  
**Ownership:** Rented from Nottinghamshire County Cricket Club  
**Land Value:** £2,000-3,000  
**Annual Rent:** £200-350 (estimated, shared with cricket)  

**Historical Summary:**
Notts County, established in 1862 (world's oldest professional football club), began playing at Trent Bridge in 1883, a venue primarily known for cricket. First home Football League match was October 6, 1888, drawing 3-3 against Blackburn Rovers. Won FA Cup in 1894 with 4-1 victory over Bolton Wanderers. Shared use with cricket club presented challenges - cricket often took precedence, leading Notts County to play early and late-season fixtures at alternative venues. In 1908, trustees decided not to renew lease, prompting move to Meadow Lane in 1910.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Bridge

### 12. Stoke - Victoria Ground

**Stadium Name:** Victoria Ground  
**Built Year:** 1878  
**Capacity (1888-89):** 5,000-10,000 (estimated)  
**Estimated Value (1888):** £3,500-5,500  
**Ownership:** Likely rented/leased  
**Land Value:** £1,500-2,500  
**Annual Rent:** £150-250 (estimated)  

**Historical Summary:**
The Victoria Ground served as Stoke's home from 1878 until 1997, marking 119 years of football history. At the time of its demolition, it was the oldest operational ground in the Football League. First league match was September 8, 1888, a 2-0 defeat against West Bromwich Albion. In 1925, Stoke-on-Trent was granted city status, prompting the club to change its name to Stoke City Football Club. Record attendance was 51,380 in March 1937. Relocated to Britannia Stadium (now bet365 Stadium) in 1997.

**Wikipedia Link:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Ground

## Implementation Notes

1. **Value Estimation:** For stadiums without exact values, use capacity-based estimation:
   - Base: £400-600 per 1,000 capacity
   - Adjust for age (older = slightly lower)
   - Adjust for facilities (basic = lower, developed = higher)

2. **Rent Estimation:** For rented stadiums:
   - Basic: £100-200/year
   - Developed: £200-350/year
   - Shared facilities: Lower rent

3. **Land Value:** Typically 30-50% of total stadium value if owned

4. **Historical Accuracy:** Prioritize documented values, estimate conservatively when unknown
