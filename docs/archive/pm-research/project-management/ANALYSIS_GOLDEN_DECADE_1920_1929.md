## 🏛️ Executive Summary

The 1920s transformed football into a modern mass-media spectacle. The decade is bookended by the recovery from WWI and the **1929 Wall Street Crash**, but the core is defined by the **1925 Offside Rule Change**—the most significant tactical shift in history—and the **1927 first radio broadcast**, which killed the information lag for the first time.

## 2. TACTICAL ENGINE 2.0: THE "1925 REVOLUTION"

This is a mandatory script for the 1925/26 season.

*   **The Change:** Required defenders between attacker and goal drops from **3 to 2**.
*   **Engine Impact:** 
    *   **Goal Frequency:** Increase average goals per match by ~40%.
    *   **The W-M Formation:** Managers will begin abandoning the 2-3-5 for the W-M (3-2-2-3).
    *   **Center Back Importance:** The "Center-Half" moves back to become a "Third Back" (Center-Back). Players' tactical familiarity will drop as they learn new roles.

## 3. MEDIA & COMMUNICATION (1920-1929)

### 3.1 The "Box" (Radio) arrives (1927)
*   **The Milestone:** Jan 22, 1927 (Arsenal vs Sheffield Utd).
*   **Information Lag:** Becomes **ZERO** for radio listeners. 
*   **Gameplay Impact:**
    *   **The "Grid System":** The Radio Times published a pitch grid (Squares 1-8). Commentary text in the inbox can reference these (e.g., "The ball is in Square 4!").
    *   **Radio Fees:** Clubs begin negotiating "Broadcasting Rights" (early revenue stream).

### 3.2 Global Press (1920s)
*   ***Kicker* (Germany, 1920):** New media source for German news.
*   ***El Gráfico* (Argentina):** Becomes the definitive South American source.
*   **Telegrams vs Radio:** Newspapers start losing the "Race for Results." Managers get news faster but with less detail than the weekly papers.
