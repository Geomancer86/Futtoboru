## History (game text)
Notts County date their birth to 1862, when a group of Nottingham gentlemen began meeting to kick a ball about in The Park; they organised properly in December 1864. That makes Notts the oldest club in the Football League, and their meetings with Nottingham Forest, first played in 1866, one of the oldest fixtures in the game.

Notts were an amateur club for twenty years, and among the strongest. Harry Cursham's FA Cup goals for the club are still a competition record, and Harwood Greenhalgh was the club's first England player in 1872. In 1885, when the FA legalised professionalism, Notts registered six professionals straight away.

In 1888 the club, then playing in chocolate and blue at the Trent Bridge cricket ground, joined the new Football League. Its sides mixed Nottinghamshire cricketers like William Gunn and Mordecai Sherwin with professionals from Scotland and the local works teams.

## 1888-89 season
A difficult first League season. Albert Moore scored the club's first League goal in a 2-1 defeat at Everton, but Notts lost 9-1 at Aston Villa and 7-0 at home to Preston, and conceded 73 goals, the worst record in the League. The bright spot was 27 October, when Bob Jardine became the first man to score five in a League match in a 6-1 win over Burnley. Harry Daft and Jardine shared top scoring with nine each, and Alf Shelton missed only one League game. The cup brought more joy: four qualifying wins and a 2-0 defeat of Old Brightonians before a 3-2 loss at The Wednesday. Sherwin, the 17-stone cricketer-goalkeeper, played his last game in December and Cursham announced his retirement in January. Notts finished 11th, ahead of Stoke only on goal average. ## Key moments to ~1895
- 1862 / 1864: traditional founding; formal organisation 7 Dec 1864 [1]
- 1866: first match against Nottingham Forest [1]
- 1872: Harwood Greenhalgh, the club's first England player [1]
- 1880: chocolate and blue halves; move to the Castle Ground [1]
- 1883: move to Trent Bridge [1]
- 1885: six professionals registered [1]
- 1888: founder member of the Football League; 11th [1] [2]
- 1889: Harry Daft wins his first England cap (2 March) [4]
- 1890: black and white stripes appear [1]
- 1891: FA Cup final, lost 1-3 to Blackburn a week after beating them 7-1 in the League [1]
- 1893: first relegation [1]
- 1894: FA Cup winners, 4-1 v Bolton at Goodison (Jimmy Logan hat-trick), the first second-tier winners [1]
