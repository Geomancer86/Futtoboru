# Crewe Alexandra (`crewe-alexandra`)
The railway-works club, FA Cup semi-finalists in 1888 and founders of the rival Combination.

| Fact | 1888-89 | Source |
|---|---|---|

## History (game text)
Crewe Alexandra grew from the cricket club of the railway works and play beside the station. They reached the FA Cup semi-final in 1888, helped by a protest about a low crossbar that overturned a defeat by Swifts. Their secretary J. G. Hall led the clubs left out of the League into the Combination, and Crewe's own ground was picked for the 1889 Cup semi-final. On the pitch, 1888-89 brought a heavy Cup replay defeat by Halliwell.

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1877: club formed; first game 1 Dec 1877 v Basford, 1-1 [1]
- 1883: first FA Cup tie, lost 0-10 to Queen's Park [1]
- Feb 1886: William Bell becomes the first Crewe international (Wales) [1]
- 1887-88: crossbar protest v Swifts; FA Cup semi-final [1][2]
- 1888-89: The Combination; lose 1-5 to Halliwell in a Cup replay [1][3]
- 1889: founder member of the Football Alliance [1][4]
- 5 Mar 1892: John Pearson wins Crewe's only full England cap [1]
- 1892: founder member of the Second Division (lose League status 1896) [1]

## Data notes
- Founded 1877 and the ground match.
- The Combination's launch: the Witton article dates the Crewe meeting to 27 Apr 1888 [4]; the Crewe article says early 1889 [1]. 1888 fits a 1888-89 league.
- Squad: William Bell (Wales, 5 caps 1881-86; 6 Combination games in 1888-89) [6] and John Pearson (whole career at Crewe, joined 1881 aged 13) [7] confirmed.
