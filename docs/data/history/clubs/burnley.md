# Burnley (`burnley`)
A former rugby club from a cotton town that hired Scots by the trainload and got a royal visit before it got a league.

## History (game text)
Burnley began life as Burnley Rovers, a rugby club. In May 1882 its members voted to take up the association game, dropped the "Rovers" and, early in 1883, accepted the cricket club's invitation to play at Turf Moor, where the club has stayed ever since.

Burnley moved fast and broke rules doing it. The committee paid players, signed a stream of Scots and named Dan Friel captain. When the Football Association still banned professionals, Burnley led the breakaway British Football Association in 1884, and the FA gave way and legalised pay in 1885.

In 1886 Prince Albert Victor watched Burnley play Bolton at Turf Moor, the first royal to visit a football ground, and the club marked it by wearing the Prince of Wales's arms on its shirts. Two years later Burnley were one of the twelve founders of the Football League.

## 1888-89 season
Burnley lost 5-2 at Preston on opening day, Gallocher scoring their first League goal, then won 4-3 at Bolton the next week with William Tait scoring the League's first hat-trick. Tait was gone by late October after a drinking episode and a missed match. The defence leaked goals: 6-1 at Notts County (where Bob Jardine scored five) and a 7-1 home thrashing by Blackburn, a game in which forward Fred Poland is said to have kept goal (single source). Walter Cox arrived in goal in November, William McKay came from Hearts and finished as top League scorer, and a winter run including a 4-0 win over Aston Villa and a 2-2 draw with the unbeaten champions Preston before 10,000 at Turf Moor kept Burnley clear of the bottom. Gallocher left for Accrington in March. In the same month Jack Yates became Burnley's first England player, scoring a hat-trick against Ireland. [4] [6] [7] [8] [10]

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1882: Burnley Rovers switch codes and become Burnley [1]
- 1883: move to Turf Moor; first professionals; Dr Dean's Cup won [2]
- 1884-85: lead the British Football Association breakaway; professionalism legalised 1885 [2]
- 1885-86: first FA Cup entry, a reserve side loses 11-0 to Darwen Old Wanderers (record defeat) [2]
- 1886: Prince Albert Victor at Turf Moor [2]
- 1888: founder member of the Football League; 9th [2] [4]
- 1889: Jack Yates scores a hat-trick on his England debut (2 March) [7]
- 1890: first Lancashire Cup, 2-0 over Blackburn Rovers; part of the first penalty kick taken (friendly v Darwen, September 1890) [2]
- 1891-92: record League win, 9-0 v Darwen [2]
- 1894: Harry Bradshaw appointed first manager [2] [5]

## Data notes
- Club record (founded 1882, Turf Moor) matches the sources.
- `turf-moor` description says the first League match was 6 Oct 1888 v Bolton (4-1): that is the first League match **at Turf Moor**; Burnley's first League game was 8 Sep 1888 at Preston [4].
- William Tait's article puts his hat-trick in Burnley's "first home game"; the season page and McFettridge's article put the 4-3 win at Bolton's Pike's Lane on 15 Sep 1888 [4] [9]. A fan history credits Tait with two goals, not three [10]. Keep "first League hat-trick" but flag it.
- Nationality: `sandy-lang-1865` (born Bridge of Weir) [13], `bill-mcfettridge-1865` (born Govan) [14], `walter-cox-1865` (Scottish keeper) [15] and `ross-mcmahon-1865` (Scottish) [16] are stored as england; sources say Scotland.
- Birth years: `thomas-midgley-1860` born 1856 in Leeds, died 1957 [17]; `william-bury-1865` born Q3 1864 per infobox (1865 in the lead), Darwen [18]; `bill-mcfettridge` 22 Apr 1864, one source 1862 [14].
- Goalkeepers: William Smith 3 apps (incl. opening day), Robert Kay 5 (from 29 Sep), Walter Cox 13 (from 10 Nov) [19] [20] [15]; one game unaccounted (Poland, per [10]).
- Alec Brady / William Brady apps are mixed up in the records; both pages say the split is unresolved [21] [22].
- `william-tait-1864` played only 5 League games (5 goals) [9]; `thomas-midgley` and `robert-duckworth` 1 each.
