## History (game text)
St George's began in Aston in 1875 and moved west to Smethwick when Harry Mitchell, son of the Cape Hill brewery owner, gave them a home and jobs for players. By 1888 the press counted them among the four best teams in the Midlands, but the League wanted one club per town and Aston Villa had Birmingham. Dropping "Mitchell" from the name, they joined the Combination and reached the last eight of the Cup before losing at Preston.

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1875: St George's founded in Aston [1]
- 1884: beat West Bromwich Albion for the Staffordshire Cup [1]
- 1886: Dennis Hodgetts leaves for Aston Villa; Cape Hill ground opens [1]
- 1888: win the Derby County Charity Cup [1]
- 2 Mar 1889: FA Cup quarter-final, lost 0-2 at Preston [2]
- 1889: League application gets 5 votes; founder member of the Football Alliance [1]
- 1890-91: best Alliance finish, 4th; Jack Devey leaves for Villa [1]
- 1891: brewery withdraws its backing; 1891-92 bottom of the Alliance [1]
- 1892: club disbands [1]

## Data notes
- Founded 1875 matches.
- Name for 1888-89: Birmingham St George's.
- The club article gives a record crowd of 12,000 v Notts County, "FA Cup 1st round, 18 January 1889"; that date does not fit the 1888-89 draw (R1 was 2 Feb, v Long Eaton), so it is probably the 1889-90 tie [1][2]. Not used.
- Squad: Billy Siddons (joined for 1888-89) and Jack Devey (1889-91, nearby) only; others placeholders. Other men named for the period: goalkeeper Harry Stansbie (1886-87), George Kinsey (to Wolves Jan 1891), Harry Davies [1].
