# Blackburn Rovers (`blackburn-rovers`)
Three FA Cups in a row (1884-86): the old cup kings of the cotton town, now chasing League glory with a mix of veterans and young goalscorers.

## History (game text)
Rovers were founded in 1875 by two young men just back from Shrewsbury School, and they wore the blue and white halves that are still the club's colours. They had no fixed home at first. In 1881 they built a proper ground at Leamington Road, with a grandstand for about 700 people.

Blackburn was a football-mad mill town. Rovers were the first provincial club to reach an FA Cup Final, in 1882, and then won the Cup three years running, 1884 to 1886. Their Scots included Fergus Suter, a Glasgow stonemason who came over from Darwen in 1880 and is often called the first professional footballer.

In 1888 Rovers were founder members of the Football League. They were unbeaten at Leamington Road in its first season and finished fourth.

## 1888-89 season
Rovers' first League game was a 5-5 home draw with Accrington. The season stayed high-scoring: 7-1 at Burnley with a Jack Southworth hat-trick, and 4-4 with Bolton. The low point was a 6-1 defeat at Villa in October. Southworth, a violinist off the field, was top scorer with 21 goals. Herby Arthur and Jimmy Forrest gave the side experience, and the club's first Scottish professional, Fergus Suter, made one last League appearance on 22 December. In the Cup, Rovers took revenge on Villa in round three, winning 8-1 in front of 12,000 at Leamington Road: Southworth scored four and Haresnape three. Wolves stopped them in the semi-final after a replay at Crewe. [2] [7] [8] [10]

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1875: Club founded (5 November); first match at Church (18 December) [1]
- 1878: Founder member of the Lancashire FA (28 September); match under artificial light v Accrington at Alexandra Meadows (4 November) [1] [2]
- 1880: Fergus Suter joins from Darwen [6]
- 1881: Moves to Leamington Road [3]
- 1882: FA Cup Final, lost 0-1 to Old Etonians; the first provincial finalist [1]
- 1884-1886: FA Cup winners three years running (Queen's Park 2-1, Queen's Park 2-0, West Bromwich Albion after a replay) [1] [2]
- 1885, 1887: England internationals played at Leamington Road [3]
- 1888: Founder member of the Football League [2]
- 1890: Moves to Ewood Park; FA Cup winners, 6-1 v The Wednesday, with William Townley scoring the first Cup Final hat-trick [1] [2] [11]
- 1891: FA Cup winners, 3-1 v Notts County [2]
- 1891: At Burnley (December) the Rovers players do not come out for the second half except goalkeeper Herby Arthur; the referee abandons the game [12]
