## History (game text)
Bolton Wanderers began in 1874 as the football team of Christ Church in Bolton, started by the curate and the master of the church school. After falling out with the vicar the players left the church grounds in 1877 and, having moved between three pitches in four years, took the name Wanderers.

From 1880-81 they settled at Pike's Lane, a rough ground with banked sides that players and spectators alike grumbled about. Under secretary J. J. Bentley, a sharp-penned football journalist and one of the men who helped William McGregor build the Football League, Bolton became one of its twelve founder members in 1888.

On the League's opening day, 8 September 1888, Bolton's local inside-right Kenny Davenport scored after a couple of minutes against Derby County, the goal now credited as the first in Football League history. Bolton still lost 6-3, but a free-scoring side led by James Brogan, Davenport and David Weir finished a respectable fifth.

## 1888-89 season
Bolton lost their first three games, the opener 3-6 at home to Derby despite Davenport's two goals, then beat Everton 6-2 with braces from Davenport, Harry Tyrer and John Milne. The autumn was patchy (a 5-1 win at West Bromwich two days after losing at Everton), but the winter brought a strong run: Weir's hat-trick beat Accrington 4-1 just before Christmas, and in March Bolton won 4-0 at Notts County and 7-3 in the return, Davenport scoring three. Five men (Brogan, Davenport, Milne, Weir, Bob Roberts) played all 22 league games [6][7]. Brogan was top scorer with 13 league goals, ahead of Davenport (11) and Weir (10) [2]. The reserves went out of the FA Cup in Belfast, 0-4 to Linfield Athletic [5].

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1874: founded as Christ Church F.C. [1]
- 1877: renamed Bolton Wanderers [1]
- 1880-81: move to Pike's Lane [1][3]
- 1884: white kit with red spots, "The Spots" [1]
- 1887-88: FA Cup first-round saga v Everton (four games); Everton disqualified for ineligible players and Bolton reinstated, then beaten 1-9 by Preston [8]
- 17 Apr 1888: founder member of the Football League [1]
- 8 Sep 1888: Davenport's goal at Pike's Lane, at 3:47 pm, credited as the League's first (credited to Aston Villa's Gershom Cox as an own goal until 2013) [6]
- 28 Mar 1891: Pike's Lane league record crowd, 14,000 v Blackburn Rovers [3]
- Apr 1892: Pike's Lane hosts the first Football League v Scottish League match [3]
- 24 Feb 1894: Pike's Lane record crowd, 20,000 v Liverpool (FA Cup) [3]
- 1894: first FA Cup final, lost 1-4 to Notts County at Goodison Park; Di Jones captain [1][9]
- 13 Apr 1895: last game at Pike's Lane, 5-0 v West Bromwich Albion; move to Burnden Park [3]
