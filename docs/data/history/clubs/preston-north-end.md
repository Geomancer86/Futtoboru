## History (game text)
North End started out as a Preston cricket club in 1863. In 1875 it leased a field at Deepdale Farm, and it is still there today. Rugby was tried and dropped. Association football was first played in 1878, and in 1880 the members voted to make it the club's main winter game.

The club grew under William Sudell, a mill manager and the club's chairman. In the early 1880s he brought skilled Scots to Preston and found them jobs at his mill. In 1884 Upton Park complained that Preston were paying players, and North End withdrew from the FA Cup. Sudell then pushed hard for professionalism, and the FA legalised it in 1885.

By 1888 Preston were the team to beat. They had won an FA Cup tie 26-0 against Hyde in 1887 and reached the 1888 Cup Final. In the League's first season they did not lose a match, and they won the Cup without conceding a goal. That made them the first side to win the "Double".

## 1888-89 season
North End opened with a 5-2 home win over Burnley on 8 September. Dewhurst and Gordon scored on their League debuts. Two 7-0 wins followed: at home to Stoke (6 Oct) and at Notts County (3 Nov), where Gordon scored a hat-trick. Only four teams took a point off them: Accrington (0-0), Aston Villa (1-1), and Burnley and Blackburn (both 2-2). John Goodall led the scoring with 21 league goals (his own article says 20) and Jimmy Ross scored 19. James Trainer kept goal in the League. For the Cup, the amateur doctor Robert Mills-Roberts played instead, and Preston went five ties without conceding. In the semi-final David Russell's goal beat West Brom. In the Final at the Oval, Fred Dewhurst (15'), Ross (25') and Sam Thomson (70') beat Wolves 3-0. Dewhurst captained the side that day. [6] [7] [8] [11] [12] [16]

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1863: Cricket club formed; moves to Moor Park and takes the "North End" name [2]
- 1875: Deepdale field leased (21 January) [1]
- 1878: First recorded association match, v Eagley at Deepdale (5 October) [1]
- 1880: Football formally adopted (May) [1]
- 1884: Withdraws from the FA Cup after Upton Park's complaint about paid players [1] [3]
- 1887: Beats Hyde 26-0 in the FA Cup; still the English first-class record [1]
- 1888: Loses the FA Cup Final to West Bromwich Albion; founder member of the Football League [1] [15]
- 1889: League and FA Cup "Double", unbeaten in the League [6]
- 1889-90: League champions again; record 10-0 league win over Stoke (14 Sep 1889) [2] [3]
- 1890-91 to 1892-93: League runners-up three times in a row [1] [2]
- 1893: Sudell loses control and the club becomes a limited company [1]
- 1895: Sudell convicted of embezzling £5,326 from the mill to fund the club; three-year sentence [5]
- 1895: Fred Dewhurst dies aged 31 (21 April) [11]

## Data notes
- `clubs.json` founded 1880 is right for the football club. The parent cricket club dates from 1863 and Deepdale from 1875 [1] [2].
- `jack-edwards-1867`: country is **scotland** in the data, but he was English, John Bentley Edwards, **b. 25 Aug 1867, Preston** [13]. The season article also lists him as ENG [6].
- `jack-gordon-1861`: the data matches the infobox (28 Apr 1861, Bridge of Weir). The Wikipedia article title says "born 1863" [14].
- `george-drummond-1865`: sourced b. 9 Jan 1865, Edinburgh [15]. `willie-graham-1866`: b. Jan 1866, Dreghorn [17]. `jock-inglis-1859`: b. 17 Jun 1859, Kilwinning [18]. `sandy-robertson-1860`: b. Dec 1860, Edinburgh [19]. `richard-whittle-1864`: b. late 1864, Preston [20].
- `david-russel-1863`: the id is spelled **Russel**; the name is **Russell** (David Kennedy Russell, b. 6 Jul 1863, Beith) [16]. Spartacus gives 1862 [21]. Do not confuse him with Davie Russell (b. 1871), the Scotland cap who was at Preston in 1892-93 [21].
- `archie-goodall-1865`: 2 apps, 1 goal for Preston before joining Villa on 1 Oct 1888 [6] [22]. This matches the data note.
- Appearance conflicts: Inglis 1 app/1 goal (season article [6]) vs 3 apps/2 goals (player article [18]). Johnny Graham 4 FA Cup apps [6] vs all 5 [23]. The Invincibles article credits Russell with 19 goals [3], which looks like a mix-up with Ross; the season article has Russell on 1 [6].
- Final attendance: 25,000 [6] vs 27,000 [7] vs 22,250 (Wolves season article [24]).
- Stoke 7-0: the season table dates it 6 Oct; the infobox says 12 Nov [6].
- Squad is complete against the season article (18 players). Nick Ross, a famous Invincible, was **not** at Preston in 1888-89 (he was at Everton; returned 1889). Single source / check [25].
