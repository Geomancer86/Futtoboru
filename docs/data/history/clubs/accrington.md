## History (game text)
Accrington Football Club was formed in 1878 at a meeting in a town pub and made its home on the cricket club's field at Thorneyholme Road, where the footballers took the western end. They played in red, which gave them the name the Owd Reds.

The club was ambitious early: it was thrown out of the Football Association in 1883 for paying a player, a year before the wider row over professionalism came to a head. By 1888 it was strong enough to be chosen among the twelve founders of the Football League, the smallest town of the twelve.

Accrington held their own in the first seasons, with England half-back George Haworth at their heart and a teenage forward, Johnny Kirkham, who would become the club's record appearance maker and scorer. Their best finish, sixth, came in 1889-90.

## 1888-89 season
Accrington opened with a 1-2 defeat at Everton, then drew 5-5 at Blackburn Rovers, where 19-year-old Kirkham scored twice on debut. Bob Brand's hat-trick, the club's first in the League, beat Derby County 6-2 on 13 October [4][6], and on 20 October goalkeeper Johnny Horne's defence held Preston North End's "Invincibles" to their only scoreless game of the season [7]. Draws were the theme (eight of them) and Accrington finished seventh on 20 points, level with Everton [4]. Billy Barbour (13) and Kirkham (12) led the scoring [4]; Horne and full-back John Stevenson played every league and cup game [7][8]. In the Cup, Blackburn were held 1-1 at Thorneyholme Road but won the replay 5-0 [4].

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1878: founded; settles at Thorneyholme Road [1][2]
- 1881: beat Blackburn Park Road 6-4 in the Lancashire Senior Cup final [1]
- 1883: expelled from the FA for paying a player [1]
- 1887-88: FA Cup run, 11-0 v Rossendale, 3-2 v Burnley, out 1-3 to Blackburn Rovers [9]
- 17 Apr 1888: founder member of the Football League [1]
- 1889-90: best finish, 6th; Haworth captain [1][10]
- 2 Jan 1893: ground record, 10,000 v Blackburn Rovers [2]
- 22 Apr 1893: relegated after losing a test match 0-1 to Sheffield United at Trent Bridge; resign from the League rather than play in Division Two, the first founder to leave for good [1]
- 1893-95: Lancashire League (4th, 12th) [1]
- 14 Jan 1896: lose 0-12 to Darwen in the Lancashire Senior Cup and fold [1]
