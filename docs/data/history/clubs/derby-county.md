## History (game text)
Derby County was born in 1884 in the offices of Derbyshire County Cricket Club, which had just lost every Championship match and needed winter income. Committee man William Morley proposed a football section, the county FA turned down "Derbyshire County" as too long, and the new club took the cricket club's amber, chocolate and blue and its ground at the Racecourse.

The start was rough: a 6-0 friendly defeat at Great Lever and a 7-0 home loss to Walsall Town in the club's first FA Cup tie. But a cup win over Aston Villa in 1885-86 made Derby's name, and helped earn them a seat among the League's twelve founders in 1888.

The early sides were full of local men and cricketers, from Derbyshire captain William Chatterton to future Wisden cricketer Levi Wright, with Scottish centre-forward Alex Higgins leading the line.

## 1888-89 season
Derby opened the League on 8 September 1888 at Bolton, came back from 3-0 down and won 6-3; George Bakewell scored the club's first League goal and 17-year-old Harry Plackett was the youngest player in the League. Then came a club-record eight straight defeats between September and December, including 5-0 losses at West Bromwich and Preston and 6-2 at Everton and Accrington. The spring was better: Higgins scored four in a 5-2 win over Aston Villa on 9 March, and Derby won 5-3 at Notts County a week later. Lol Plackett was the only ever-present and Higgins top-scored with 11 League goals. Derby finished 10th. [1] [4] [5] [6] [7]

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1884: first recorded game, 0-6 at Great Lever (13 September); first FA Cup tie, 0-7 v Walsall Town [1]
- 1885-86: FA Cup wins over Birmingham St George's and Aston Villa [1]
- 1888: founder member of the Football League; 10th [1] [4]
- 1889: John Goodall signs from Preston (May) [1]
- 1891: absorb Derby Midland; Goodall becomes the first Derby player capped for England while at the club (March 1891) [1]
- 1892: Steve Bloomer's League debut, 3-1 at Stoke [1]
- 1894: change to black and white [1]
- 1895: move to the Baseball Ground [1]
