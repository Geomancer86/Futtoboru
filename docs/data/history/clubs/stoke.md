## History (game text)
Stoke trace their roots to railway apprentices who had learned the game at Charterhouse School; the club calls 1863 its birth year, though the first match on record is from 1868. In 1878 they merged with the Stoke Victoria Athletic Club and moved to the Victoria Ground, which would be home for more than a century.

Stoke won the first two Staffordshire Cups (1877, 1878), turned professional in August 1885 and won their first FA Cup tie, 10-0 against Caernarfon Wanderers, in 1886-87. Players started on half a crown a week and won a rise to five shillings by threatening to strike.

When the Football League was formed in 1888, Stoke's secretary-manager Harry Lockett went to the London meeting and came back as the League's first secretary. On the pitch, the club found the new competition hard going.

## 1888-89 season
Stoke opened the League with a 2-0 home defeat by West Bromwich Albion before 4,524, and Frank Staton scored the club's first League goal in a 5-1 loss at Aston Villa. The first win, 3-0 over Notts County, came in week three with two goals from Billy Tunnicliffe. On 6 October the first team lost 7-0 at Preston with two borrowed Preston reserves after two men missed the train, while the reserves lost an FA Cup tie to non-League Warwick County on the same day. A 4-3 win over Burnley, a 3-0 double over Notts County and a 2-1 win over Blackburn were the high points. Captain Tommy Clare, Alf Underwood and goalkeeper Bill Rowley held the defence together, and Scottish forward Bob McSkimming played every game and top-scored with six. Stoke finished bottom on goal average. [3] [7] [8] [9]

- 1868: first documented match (17 October) [1]
- 1877-78: Staffordshire Cup winners in its first two seasons; record 26-0 v Mow Cop [1]
- 1878: merger, renamed Stoke, move to the Victoria Ground [1]
- 1885: professionalism (August) [1]
- 1888: founder member of the Football League; Lockett becomes League secretary [1] [4]

- 1889-90: bottom again, including a 10-0 defeat at Preston; not re-elected in 1890, Sunderland take the place [1] [4]
