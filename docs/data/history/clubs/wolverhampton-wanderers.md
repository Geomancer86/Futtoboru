## History (game text)
Wolves started out in 1877 as a schoolboys' team at St Luke's in Blakenhall, playing with a ball their headmaster had given them. Two years later they joined up with the local Wanderers cricket club and took the town's name. In 1881 they moved to Dudley Road, across from the Fighting Cocks Inn.

The club stayed local. Its founders, Baynton and Brodie, were still in the side in 1888, and secretary Jack Addenbrooke had taught at St Luke's. In 1884 Wolves won the Wrekin Cup, their first trophy. By 1888 they were strong enough to be one of the twelve founders of the Football League, and they finished third in its first season.

## 1888-89 season
Wolves were at home to Aston Villa on 8 September, one of the League's opening fixtures, and drew 1-1. Their goal was an own goal by Villa's Gershom Cox. In their next home game Preston beat them 4-0, but over the season they were one of the most consistent sides, with a 5-0 win over Everton and doubles over West Brom and Stoke. Harry Wood was top scorer with 13 league goals, and Dickie Baugh and Harry Allen played every league match. The Cup run was the high point: five ties, a record 10,000 crowd at Dudley Road for The Wednesday, and a semi-final win over the old Cup kings Blackburn after a replay at Crewe. In the Final at the Oval, with Brodie as captain, they lost 3-0 to the Invincibles. [6] [7] [8]

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1877: St Luke's F.C. formed in Blakenhall [1] [2]
- 1879: Merger with Blakenhall Wanderers; becomes Wolverhampton Wanderers (August) [2]
- 1881: Moves to Dudley Road [1]
- 1883: First FA Cup tie, 4-1 v Long Eaton Rangers; Brodie scores the first Cup goal [9]
- 1884: Wrekin Cup, the first trophy [5] [9]
- 1885: Jack Addenbrooke becomes secretary-manager (August) [4]
- 1888: Founder member of the Football League [1]
- 1889: FA Cup Final, lost 0-3 to Preston; John Brodie captains England on his debut; move to Molineux (summer) [2] [9]
- 1891-92: Old gold and black registered as club colours [3]
- 1893: FA Cup winners, 1-0 v Everton at Fallowfield, Manchester; Harry Allen scores [1] [10]
- 1895: David Wykes (7 Oct) and Harry Allen (23 Feb) die young [10] [11]

## Data notes
- `dudley-road` description: correct on dates, first League match and the 10,000 record v The Wednesday [6]. Some sources say Harper's Field was "James Harper's" [1], others "John Harper's" [2].
- R3 score v The Wednesday: 3-0 (season article [6]) vs 5-0 (1889 final article [7]). Final attendance: 22,250 [6] vs 25,000 (Preston season article) vs 27,000 [7]. Semi-final date: 16 Mar [6] vs 19 Mar (Blackburn season article).
- `jack-baynton-1859`: the article says he may have been born in 1860 [5]. He left for Kidderminster Olympic in April 1889 [5].
- `albert-fletcher-1867`: birthplace given as both Nottingham and Wolverhampton [12]. `david-wykes-1867`: Wolverhampton vs Walsall [11].
- `tommy-hunter-1863`: the article dates his signing to Aug 1886, while its infobox has 1888-89 only [13].
- All 19 squad ids match the season article. `r-dudley-1865` (1 app), `t-tomkyes-1866` (1 app), `john-benton-1866` (1 app), `nick-anderson-1867` (2 apps): appearances sourced; birth years not sourced (game estimates).
- Wolves did not play at Molineux until 1889-90, so `dudley-road` is correct for 1888-89 [2].
