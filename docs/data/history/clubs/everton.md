## History (game text)
Everton began in 1878 as St Domingo's, the football side of a Methodist chapel in the Everton district of Liverpool, and in November 1879 the members met at the Queen's Head and renamed the club after their neighbourhood. They played first on an open pitch in Stanley Park, then at Priory Road, until the crowds grew too big and too noisy for the landlord.

In 1884 they moved to a field at Anfield, soon bought by the brewer and Tory alderman John Houlding. Stands went up, gates swelled, and Everton became one of the best-supported clubs in England. Money brought professionals, some of them from Scotland, and trouble: in 1887-88 the FA threw Everton out of the Cup and suspended them for a month for fielding ineligible players.

Everton were founder members of the Football League in 1888. They spent heavily on new men such as Nick Ross from Preston and young Edgar Chadwick from Blackburn, and used 35 players in the first season, still a club record. Within three seasons they were champions, and within four they had left Anfield after a rent dispute with Houlding, who founded Liverpool to fill it.

## 1888-89 season
Everton won their first two games at Anfield, George Fleming scoring both goals in the 2-1 win over Accrington on opening day, but were beaten 6-2 at Bolton [10][11]. October brought the best spell, a 2-0 home win over Aston Villa and two wins over Derby (4-2 away, then 6-2 at home with an Alex McKinnon hat-trick) [10]. The defence was weakened when right-back Alec Dick was suspended for striking an opponent at Notts County in November [12][2]. After a 3-2 win over Burnley in late November Everton won only twice more; 15,000 saw Preston win 2-0 at Anfield in January [6][10]. They finished eighth, one place clear of re-election [6]. Chadwick, ever-present, was top league scorer with six [13].

## Key moments to ~1895
- 1878: St Domingo's F.C. formed [1]
- Nov 1879: renamed Everton at the Queen's Head Hotel [1]
- 1882/83: move to Priory Road [1]
- 1884: move to Anfield [1]
- 1887-88: FA Cup v Bolton (four games); Everton disqualified and suspended for a month for fielding seven ineligible players [14]
- 17 Apr 1888: founder member of the Football League [1]
- 1888-89: withdraw from the FA Cup; 35 players used in the League [6][8]
- 1889-90: League runners-up [1]
- Mar 1890: Johnny Holt becomes Everton's first England international [15]
- 1890-91: League champions by two points; Fred Geary top scorer [1]
- Jan-Jun 1892: rent dispute with Houlding; last Anfield game 18 Apr 1892 v Bolton; move to Goodison Park; Houlding's company becomes Liverpool F.C. [1]
- 26 Mar 1893: FA Cup final, lost 0-1 to Wolves at Fallowfield, Manchester [1]
