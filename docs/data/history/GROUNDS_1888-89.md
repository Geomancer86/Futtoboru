# Grounds of 1888-89

The 12 Football League grounds as they were in 1888-89, plus Notts County's second ground and the main FA Cup
venues and entrants. Each section has a summary, a facts table with a source per row, and what the illustration
(`tools/art`) draws. Researched 2026-11-27 (lane H-02).

**How sure we are.** No period newspaper text could be fetched directly: Welsh Newspapers Online, Papers Past,
Trove and the British Newspaper Archive all refused automated access. The best near-period material is in the
transcribed Liverpool papers on bluecorrespondent.co.uk and playupliverpool.com (Anfield), Tony Baxter's
oldvelodromes.co.uk (it quotes local papers on the athletics and cycling grounds), Lenton Times (Gregory Ground)
and club history pages. Most structural facts on Wikipedia come from one book: Smith & Smith, *The Ultimate
Directory of English & Scottish Football League Grounds* (2005), shortened here to **UD**.

- **(snippet)**: the fact was seen only in a search-result summary because the page itself was blocked. Treat it
  as weaker.
- Attendances are press estimates. The 1888-89 match figures are in `docs/data/results_1888-89.csv`; they are not
  repeated here unless they set a record.
- "Not found" means we looked and found nothing. It does not mean the thing did not exist.

**Corrections for the game data** (proposed, not applied; see `docs/design/GROUNDS_DATA_PROPOSAL.md`):

- `racecourse-ground` (Derby County) has the Baseball Ground's story in its `description`.
- Notts Rangers had left the Castle Ground for Meadow Lane by Sep 1886.
- Grimsby Town were at Clee Park, not Abbey Park, until summer 1889.
- Chatham Town played the 1889 tie on the Army's open "Lines", not at Maidstone Road.
- `wellington-road` has `capacity` 26,849. That figure is the 1888 record crowd, not a capacity.

## Football League

### deepdale
**Deepdale, Preston (Preston North End).** A former farm on the edge of Preston, leased in 1875 by the North End
cricket club. It was used for cricket and rugby, then association football from 1878. By 1888-89 it was a
multi-sport enclosure with a railed grass running and cycling track and big annual athletics festivals. Details
of its 1888-89 stands are thin: the only stand we could document is a grandstand "newly erected" in 1876.

### wellington-road
**Wellington Road, Perry Barr (Aston Villa).** A rough field that was Villa's home from 1876 to 1897. It had a
grandstand on the east touchline and pavilions on the west side and behind the south goal. The pitch sloped away
from one goal and was grazed, not mown. Villa held it on a short sub-lease from the Bridge Trust. On 7 Jan 1888 a
record crowd of 20,000 to 30,000 overran it for the FA Cup tie with Preston.

### dudley-road
**Dudley Road, Blakenhall (Wolverhampton Wanderers).** A basic ground opposite the Fighting Cocks Inn, south of
the town centre, used from 1881 to 1889. It had a lean-to shelter on the south touchline and a small standing area
laid with duckboards. Its last match, the FA Cup tie with The Wednesday on 2 Mar 1889, drew its record 10,000.
Wolves then moved to Molineux.

### leamington-road
**Leamington Road / "Leamington Street", Blackburn (Blackburn Rovers).** Rovers' first ground of their own,
1881-1890, on rising ground off Preston New Road. It had a grandstand on the north-east touchline, a pavilion in
the east corner, duckboards and boards round the pitch, and an ornate entrance arch. It staged England's
internationals in 1885 and 1887. Rovers moved to Ewood Park in 1890, after the lease cost went up.

### pikes-lane
**Pike's Lane, Deane (Bolton Wanderers).** A muddy field of about 4.5 acres, used for Druids' galas and
professional running before Wanderers took it at £35 a year. At first there were only embankments on both
sides. A grandstand and wooden terracing came later; the date is not found. The first Football League goal was
scored here by Kenny Davenport on 8 Sep 1888. A farmer let people watch from his overlooking field for half the
club's price.

### stoney-lane
**Stoney Lane, West Bromwich (West Bromwich Albion).** Albion's rented ground from 1885 to 1900. It had a wooden
grandstand nicknamed the "Noah's Ark" on the south touchline, flanked by open wooden stands, and raised banking
on the north side. The lease was short, so little was spent. By the late 1890s the *Free Press* called it
"perhaps the worst ground in the Football League".

### victoria-ground
**Victoria Ground, Stoke (Stoke).** Built in 1878 by the Stoke Victoria Athletic Club and named after the
nearby Victoria Hotel. It was an athletics oval with a cinder track of about 500 yards round the pitch. In
1888-89 it had grass banks at each end, a small wooden stand for 1,000 on the east (Boothen Road) side, and a bank
for 4,000 opposite. It staged England v Wales in 1889, and Wakes Week athletics drew far bigger crowds than
football did.

### racecourse-ground
**County Cricket Ground on the Racecourse, Derby (Derby County).** Derby County grew out of Derbyshire CCC in 1884
and played across the west end of the county cricket ground. That ground lay inside the southern curve of Derby
Racecourse off Nottingham Road. Spectators had the large racecourse grandstand and a joint cricket and football
pavilion built in 1885. Race meetings came first, which in the end drove the club to the Baseball Ground in
1895. **The `description` in `grounds.json` is about the Baseball Ground and should be replaced.**

### trent-bridge
**Trent Bridge, West Bridgford (Notts County).** The county cricket ground behind the Trent Bridge Inn, laid out
by William Clarke in 1838. Notts County used it for big games from the 1860s and as their usual home from 1883
to 1910. Cricket came first, so early- and late-season games went elsewhere. Its big new building was the
members' pavilion of 1886, said to be the largest in England then.

### castle-ground

track laid in 1886. Its only real spectator building was a pavilion at the north end. Notts County played here
when Trent Bridge was given over to cricket: 0-4 v Bolton on 5 Mar 1889 (about 3,000, its first League match),
and very likely 3-5 v Derby on 16 Mar 1889. **Notts Rangers moved to Meadow Lane in Sep 1886** (*Athletic News*,
7 Sep 1886), so `squads.json` has them on the wrong ground.

### thorneyholme-road
**Thorneyholme Road, Accrington (Accrington).** The ground of Accrington Cricket Club, leased from Colonel
Hargreaves of Broad Oak "for a nominal rent of one shilling per year". The football pitch was at the western
end. Football could run only from early October to the end of March, so Accrington's early 1888-89 home games were
played elsewhere. Stands: a covered stand and open seating on one touchline, and open seating behind both goals.

### anfield
**Anfield (Everton, 1884-92).** A field off Anfield Road near Stanley Park, owned by the Orrell family and
sublet to Everton through John Houlding. Players changed at Houlding's Sandon Hotel, the club headquarters. It
began in 1884 with a hoarding and a 65-yard stand that was "blown down in the first October winds". By 1888-89
it was a large enclosure: the Oakfield Road stand (late 1886, about £1,500, about 4,000) and an Anfield Road end
stand (1887). It was re-turfed into a level lawn for the first League season.

### turf-moor
**Turf Moor, Burnley (Burnley).** A former common beside Burnley Cricket Club (there since 1843), with Bee Hole
Colliery to the east. Burnley FC moved in in February 1883. In 1885 an 800-seat wooden grandstand went up on the
south side along Brunshaw Road, with open standing for about 5,000 at each end. In October 1886 Prince Albert
Victor watched a match here, the first royal visit to a football ground.

### Chatham: the Great Lines (no picture yet)
Chatham played on Army-owned open ground, "The Lines". Army rules meant no admission could be charged, so the
ground had no fence. The 17,000 for Chatham 1-10 WBA (2 Mar 1889) can only be an estimate. After this cup run
the FA required enclosed grounds with a gate share for the visitors, and Chatham moved to Maidstone Road, which
they enclosed with a seated pavilion for £125. Sources: [Chatham Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Town_F.C.);
[WBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1888%E2%80%9389_West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C._season). The venue is
unverified against a period source.

## Best period details
- Anfield "bears the resemblance of a huge circus" (*Football Field*, 28 Dec 1888). Its 1888 lawn was "perfectly
  level", and fans were told not to "race across the ground like a lot of wild Indians".
- Perry Barr: players changed in a blacksmith's shed, then the Crown & Cushion. The turf was grazed, a haystack
  had to be cleared, and the gate was 2d.
- Pike's Lane: 3d and 6d, stand extra, while a farmer sold a view from his field for half price.
- Turf Moor: 4d admission in 1888-89. The footballers left the shared dressing room "uncleaned", Bee Hole
  Colliery was next door, and a prince came in 1886.
- Thorneyholme: one shilling a year rent, and cricket pushed football out until October.
- Dudley Road: a lean-to and duckboards opposite the Fighting Cocks, and a record 10,000 on its last day.
- Gregory Ground: "Football by Wells Light", 25 Mar 1889. Chatham's free, unfenced Army ground led the FA to
  require enclosed grounds.
