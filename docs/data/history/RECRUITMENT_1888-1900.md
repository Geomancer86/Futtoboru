# Recruitment, trials and signing 1885-1900 (R-01, BOOK3PRO, 2026-11-28)

What the design ([`docs/design/RECRUITMENT_AND_TRIALS.md`](../../design/RECRUITMENT_AND_TRIALS.md)) rests on. These are research
notes, not game data. Confidence: **H** = a period source or several reliable ones agree, **M** = one reliable secondary
source, **L** = a snippet, an uncited page, or the sources conflict. `[n]` = the numbered sources at the end. The
newspaper quotes come from bluecorrespondent.co.uk transcriptions of the 1888-89 Liverpool and Lancashire press [1][2].
I checked the transcribed quotes in [1][2] against the pages myself; everything else comes from research-agent reports.

Not reached (403 or 5xx): Spartacus pages about wages, 11v11, the National Football Museum, BAILII, the ESFA history page,
PlayingPasts, and Gutenberg's copy of Bone (1890). No academic monograph (Mason, Taylor, Vamplew, Russell, Inglis) was
read directly; where a finding rests on one, it is cited through the page that quotes it.

## 1. How clubs found players
| # | Finding | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Before 1885 Lancashire clubs paid players secretly: real or nominal jobs plus cash (Blackburn Olympic 1883). Bolton were in dispute with the FA from 1880 over terms offered to Scots. | H | [3] |
| 1.2 | **Preston (Sudell):** mill manager, club chairman/secretary and in effect the manager. He made recruiting trips to Scotland from 1883 and found recruits jobs at his mill. In 1884 Upton Park protested and Preston withdrew from the Cup. Sudell was later convicted of embezzling from the mill. The sources disagree on the details: 1893-94 and £5,000 [4], or 1895 and £5,326 spent on wages [5]. | H/M | [4][5] |
| 1.3 | **The Scottish flow:** recruits were mostly about 21, first from Ayrshire and Edinburgh, later from Renton, Vale of Leven and Dumbarton. The counts disagree. One source gives about 40 Scots in England in 1883-84, 90 in 1884-85, 135 in 1888-89 and 200+ by 1890-91 [6]. Another gives 57 in 1884 (an SFA count), about 177 in 1890-91 and about 244 in 1892-93 [7]. Order of magnitude only. | L-M | [6][7] |
| 1.4 | **Adverts in the Scottish press:** the *Scottish Umpire* of 23 Oct 1888 carried a "leading Lancashire Association Club" wanting "a First-Class Centre and Right Wing Player" [8]. Clubs also advertised trials at the player's own expense [7][9]. No example of a player advertising himself was found. | H / M | [7][8][9] |
| 1.5 | **Agents:** in 1895 *Scottish Sport* complained of English "agents" and "sub-agents" placed inside Scottish clubs to "breed discontent". Blackburn's secretary spent a week in Scotland and signed Wilkie from Partick Thistle for £40 [10]. Everton's Scottish recruiting is credited to David Waugh [9]. The story of Sunderland's Tyzack scouting disguised as a priest is anecdotal and uncited [11]. | H (1895) / L | [9][10][11] |
| 1.6 | **Poaching between English clubs, summer 1888:** "a couple of Evertonians visited Preston in disguise, and ... made him [Smalley] a magnificent offer" (LEP 25 Aug 1888). Everton took Ross and Smalley from Preston and Holt from Bootle, who was reportedly sacked from his job once his signing was known. The League's 1 May 1888 meeting "fixed" registration "so that every club will have equal chances in the kidnapping business" (*Accrington Times*). | H | [1][2][12] |
| 1.7 | **Feeders:** church and Sunday-school clubs (Villa, Wolves, Everton, Bolton and Burnley all began that way; 25 of 112 Liverpool clubs in 1885 had church links), works and local clubs. Everton's 1889 reserves came from local clubs ("Hammond, formerly of Edgehill", "Howell, formerly of Stanley"). | M-H | [13][14] |
| 1.8 | **Jobs as cover:** at the "amateur" Sunderland Albion (1888), breweries gave players jobs, e.g. Hannah as landlord of the Free Gardeners Arms. WBA's Roberts was reportedly offered a pub at £200 a year in 1890. | M | [15] |

## 2. Trials and the public
| # | Finding | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **August practice matches decided who was kept:** Keys of Derby "created a big reputation in the practice matches, but has not yet been definitely retained" (C&FF 18 Aug 1888). In early friendlies "the home club tried all their new men" (*Liverpool Mercury*, 3 Sep 1888). Some 1887 Everton men were "merely on trial" in August friendlies. Fringe players went to the reserves. | H | [1][2] |
| 2.2 | **Crowds came to practice games:** "fully 4,000 spectators" turned out for Ross's first Wednesday-evening practice for Everton (LEP 18 Aug 1888); no admission charge is stated. | H | [1] |
| 2.3 | **Public trial with a gate, 1889:** Everton First Team v Reserves, Anfield, Wed 28 Aug 1889, 3-0. "Close upon three thousand people paid to admission" (*Courier*). C&FF gives a "19 pounds gate" and "four or five thousand ... and quite as many outside". The press judged the trialists in print: Hammond had "the making of a really good back", and Nidd was singled out for speed. | H (crowds conflict) | [14] |
| 2.4 | **Trial-side names:** no 1888 English source was found for "Probables v Possibles" or "Colours v Whites". The sourced form is first team v reserves. | (gap) | [14] |
| 2.5 | **Price sense:** in Feb 1891 a smaller friendly crowd was put down to league prices being charged for a friendly. | H | [16] |

## 3. Registration, eligibility, movement (rules by date)
| Date | Rule | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 Jul 1885 | Professionalism legal. A professional registers with the FA and must be **born within 6 miles of the ground or have lived there for 2 years**, and may not play for more than one club in a season without special permission. Whether the 6-mile rule applied only to Cup ties is not confirmed (L). | H | [17][3] |
| Dec 1887 | Sunderland were disqualified from the Cup for Scots, one under an assumed name. | M | [15] |
| 1888-89 | "No player shall sign a **second registration paper** in one season without the consent of the Council" (LEP 25 Aug 1888). Ross's form went to the FA and the Lancashire FA; he missed Everton's first League match waiting for approval. His release cost the repayment of "retaining fees": £17 10s, about 7 weeks of wages. Clubs met residence by having a signing "take up his residence near the ground". | H | [1][2] |
| 1889 | The residence qualification was dropped (Inglis 1988, cited). A December 1888 Liverpool conference debated payment, registration and the "birth qualification" (L, snippet). | M | [3] |
| Sep 1890 | Sunderland lost 2 points for playing Doig before his League registration had run 7 days. The period comes only from [18]. | M | [18][19] |
| 1893-94 | **Retain and transfer:** a League club keeps a player's registration even after his contract ends unrenewed. Before this, players moved freely between seasons. 1891 is not supported. | H | [20][21] |
| 1893 | Professionalism legal in Scotland (560 registered). Before that the SFA blacklisted known professionals. | H/M | [3][11] |
| 1893-94 | First notable fees: Groves, WBA to Villa, 1893, **£100** (said to be FA-ordered); Dorrell, Leicester Fosse to Villa, May 1894, £250. Goodall, Preston to Villa, Oct 1888, is called the first move between League clubs (L). | H / M / L | [22][21][23] |
| 1901 | Maximum wage £4 a week, bonuses banned (FA 1900 or 1901: sources differ). A claimed 1893 cap (£140 a year, £1 a week in summer) may have been a proposal only (L). The date of the £10 signing-on limit was not found. | H / L | [20][21][24] |

## 4. Contracts and pay
| # | Finding | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **One season:** players "signed articles to play ... during the forthcoming season" (Lewis, Aug 1888). | M-H | [1][21] |
| 4.2 | **Wages 1888:** Everton offered Holt "45s per week for two matches a week and 20s per week during the summer" (C&FF 4 Aug 1888, a reader's letter). Ross's £17 10s for 7 weeks implies about 50s a week. **Summer pay existed, lower than winter pay.** | H | [1] |
| 4.3 | **Stars:** Ross was reportedly promised to be "set up in business" (£100 is reported second-hand); a £10 a week figure is uncited (L). | L-M | [1][12] |
| 4.4 | By 1894 the League President said over 70% of gross receipts went on players' wages, outfits and travel; the press complained of summer pay. | M | [24] |
| 4.5 | **Amateurs:** a man could still move freely if he had not "signed a professional form" (Smalley, LEP 1 Sep 1888). "Broken time" in 1888 is mainly a rugby term (L). | H / L | [2] |

## 5. Youth, ages, veterans
| # | Finding | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **No youth teams or academies** in the modern sense were found. "Junior" meant a level of play, not an age group (the Scottish Junior FA was founded 2 Oct 1886). | M | [25][13] |
| 5.2 | **Schools football:** South London Schools FA 1885; Sheffield school matches and the Clegg Shield 1889; London Schools FA Oct 1892; English Schools FA 1904. Standard work: Kerrigan, *Teachers and Football* (1885-1915). | M-H | [26][27][28][29] |
| 5.3 | **Debut ages:** Plackett (Derby) debuted 8 Sep 1888 aged 17y147d, the youngest of the opening weekend; Wardle (Notts County) aged 18y248d. No 16-year-old debutant was found. | M-H | [30][31] |
| 5.4 | **Ages:** partial 1888-89 lists show players of about 21-31 (Preston's oldest, Graham, b. 1857). No man of 35+ was found. Retired players often became publicans (examples mostly after 1900), trainers (Waugh at Everton, 1889) or secretaries (Ramsay at Villa). | L-M | [32][33][34][14] |

## 6. Who judged players, and training
| # | Finding | Conf. | Src |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **The committee picked the team each week** (Villa: McGregor, Rinder; Ramsay chaired it "at one time"). Committee men also went on the training walks. | M-H | [35][36][34] |
| 6.2 | **Secretary(-manager):** Sudell (Preston); Tom Watson (Newcastle East End, then Sunderland secretary from Aug 1889, recruited Scots); Ramsay (Villa). | H | [4][37] |
| 6.3 | **The trainer:** fitness and care, not tactics. Sprinting, running, fast walking, rubbing down, examining players after practice, prescribing baths "according to condition" (1899). Often a former sprinter or pedestrian (Villa's Billy Gorman, a former sprint champion). | M-H | [34][38][39] |
| 6.4 | **The captain** taught on the field at early Villa (Ramsay "set to work to teach them dribbling") and named his successor. | M-H | [36] |
| 6.5 | **"Scout"** as a named role: no early usage was found. The function existed (agents, secretaries' trips, adverts). | (gap) | [9][10] |
| 6.6 | **Training:** Villa's 1887 cup week at Holt Fleet: sprints and runs, 8-10 mile walks, about 1.5 hours of football, rub-down, bed by 10 (Hunter 1890). Liverpool in 1899 at St Anne's: walks, baths and sprinting; Watson let the players see no ball all week. Needham (1901): start a month before the season with walking, skipping, sprints and mile runs, sometimes clubs and dumb-bells, ball work light. Everton had a practice ground (Belmont Road, 1888) and weekday-evening practice. Turkish and brine baths in 1880s football: not sourced (gap). | H | [34][38][1] |

Vamplew 1988); British Newspaper Archive searches for "trial match" in 1888 (subscription only).
