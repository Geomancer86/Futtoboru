## 1. Literacy: signing the marriage register
Signing your name (not making a mark) is the era's only national series. It **overstates** adult reading ability and
**understates** that of the young (Vincent, N) [1]. Use it as "can write his name at least".

## 5. What the game can use now
- Literacy (can write his name) by birth country and cohort: England and Wales from about 81% (men, 1872) to 97% (1899);
  Scotland about 90% (men, 1872); Ireland 74% (men, 1882), lower in Connacht; women lower until about 1900.
- Welsh at home: by county from 1891/1911 (the rural west mostly Welsh, Monmouthshire and Radnorshire mostly English).
- Gaelic: Highland counties 60-75%, the Lowland football counties under 2%. Irish: by province, highest in Connacht.
- Foreign-born: small (Germans the largest European group, about 50,000 in England and Wales 1891), concentrated in
  London, Lancashire and the ports. Real early cases were raised in Britain.
- Gaps to fill (R-11 data slice): the 1882 county marriage table [3], the 1891 Welsh county table (Vision of Britain),
  Irish county series (UK Data Service 3582), Gaelic 1891/1901 by county.
